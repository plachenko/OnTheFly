# Hybrid car prototype

Open project.godot in Godot 4.7. Hybrid reactions are now enabled by default on compatible production traffic and parked cars, including police. The sandbox remains available: open scenes/hybrid_car_demo.tscn and press F6.

## Production integration

Run the normal game with F5 and choose a driving level. Bump a Kenney car gently for small tip/recovery; hit laterally at medium speed for a stronger tip or rollover; a relative normal impact speed of 12+ m/s launches engine-controlled flight and contact-triggered explosion. Gameplay strength comes from truck velocity minus car motion, projected on the collision normal. Controller aim and the arrow remain demo-only.

traffic_car.gd instantiates the same hybrid_car.gd controller in external-owner mode, reusing the existing Visual, model and collider without a second car mesh. Route progress, intersection turns, crosswalk rules, avoidance, parked settings, horns and police pursuit remain in traffic_car.gd. All eight compatible rigged models use it: sedan, sedan-sports, hatchback-sports, taxi, suv, van, police and delivery. Production fronts already face +Z, so their orientation and scale are retained; only the demo applies its 180-degree correction. Driving pitch is mapped to production's forward convention and turn roll uses shared easing.

Moving traffic uses its existing braking/route controller before a grounded light/medium reaction captures tire anchors. The impact pulse is retained during braking, then the tip briefly pauses route movement until recovery. Parked/stopped cars react immediately. This is a traction-braking approximation, not an instantaneous freeze at cruising speed or a lateral tire-slip solver. Strong hits launch immediately and retain car motion. Truck contact damage/reporting hooks remain, with a short reaction cooldown. Boost can redirect an airborne wreck; grounded disabled cars stay anchored.

During hybrid flight the production RigidBody3D stays frozen: the shared controller moves its owner, applies gravity and mixed spin, and excludes itself and its owner from crash queries. Swept/overlapping traffic hits propagate the existing chain-hit API before the source explodes. Strong crashes explode once on contact; hybrid flight has no legacy timed fuse. Grounded rollovers disable driving without exploding. Existing fade/cleanup delays remain; grounded disabled cars fade away instead of igniting.

The hybrid_reactions_enabled export defaults to true. Set it false on the traffic-car scene before spawning to use original rigid-body wreck/suspension behavior. Models missing the required four wheel meshes and Chassis/Spring bones retain that compatibility fallback. No temporary rigid-body activation occurs inside a hybrid reaction.

Multiplayer simulation and impacts are server-owned. Prop snapshots now carry reaction state, velocity, visual/collider transforms, wheel poses, lights, explosion/disable state and cleanup time. Clients apply poses without authoring reactions; repeated explosion payloads cannot duplicate effects. Authoritative absence of a traffic prop removes its client car/collider. Existing sequence ordering remains. The expanded payload adds bandwidth; reaction poses use the existing immediate prop-snapshot application rather than new interpolation.

Implementation files: traffic_car.gd, hybrid_car.gd, truck.gd and multiplayer_game.gd. The demo interface remains in hybrid_car_demo.gd. Unrelated plugin, road-rule and city changes were preserved.

## Controls and rollover test

Keyboard: W/S forward/reverse, A/D steer, neutral throttle brakes. Q/E cycle eight horizontal push directions and up/down. Minus/plus adjust relative impact speed (1–40 m/s); Hold Space and release to apply a charged impact. 1/2/3 apply 3/11/20 m/s. 4 applies 11.8 m/s for a high-medium grounded rollover test. M/N swap models; R resets with selected model/direction/strength preserved. Hold a turn while driving for a U-turn.

Controller: left stick drives/steers. RIGHT stick continuously aims horizontal push in car-local coordinates; last aim remains on release into deadzone. RT/LT increase/decrease strength, hold A/Cross and release to apply, B/Circle resets, LB/RB swap models, D-pad up/down select vertical impact, X/Square applies the 11.8 m/s high-medium preset. The first connected pad is used; keyboard remains available. Saved GameOptions deadzones and steering sensitivity are read without changing production InputMap. No vibration/remapping is added.

To try grounded rollover, aim right or left with Q/E or the right stick, then press 4 or X/Square. Some upper-medium hits disable the car on its side/roof, depending on grip, model height, terrain bias and the estimated balance/support point. Near-boundary hits may recover; reset and repeat or try a taller model. Strength 12+ uses airborne crash behavior instead. HUD shows state, grip and roll angle. DISABLED cars ignore driving and further hits until R/B resets.

The yellow arrow shows PUSH travel, not the incoming striker's position. Up on the right stick pushes forward (-Z); forward push is a rear hit. Arrow length is 1 + 0.1 * strength meters, bounded to 1.1–5 m. It follows aim, strength, car heading and selected model; it has no collision. Strength means relative impact speed, not force in newtons.

## Held impact charge (demo only)

Space and A/Cross begin a charge on press and commit exactly once on release. The default base is now 3 m/s. Below 12 m/s, holding for up to two seconds increases effective strength linearly from the selected base to 11.8 m/s, so a tap stays light and a longer hold increases pitch/roll and can graduate to grounded rollover. Bases of 12+ remain flight impacts and charge up to 1.5 times base, capped at 40 m/s. RT/LT and keyboard minus/plus still adjust the base while holding. The arrow grows with effective strength, and HUD shows held seconds, base/effective speed, estimated target tip angle, pitch/roll, balance margin and last committed strength/world push. After release the arrow returns to the selected next-impact strength; the last committed value remains visible. Aim and heading at release determine the committed push; right-stick deadzone keeps last aim. No impacts are applied each frame during charging.

Reset, model switching and immediate preset impacts cancel pending charge. Unplugging the charging controller cancels it. Preset 1/2/3/4 and X/Square remain immediate. The charge code is confined to hybrid_car_demo.gd and does not alter truck or kitchen controls in actual gameplay.

## Shared models, chassis and tires

Six existing Kenney rigged GLBs are reused: sedan, hatchback-sports, suv, van, taxi and police in assets/kenney_city/cars_rigged. Scale is 1.8 and every imported root receives a 180-degree Y correction so its front agrees with controller -Z. All transformed mesh bounds center the model in X/Z and normalize tire height. No new assets, physics bake, Blender export or glTF round trip were generated.

Movement/heading belong to CharacterBody3D. The independent visual wrapper applies terrain orientation and body pitch/roll. Actual wheel meshes are matched to Spring_FL/FR/RL/RR bones by the existing mesh names. The SUV's rear spare is treated as body geometry and detached from its instance's Spring_RR skin assignment, preserving the source asset. Chassis and crumple bones retain their rest poses; body motion is in the wrapper, while wheel bones counter it selectively.

During grounded reactions the driving root stops to represent static tire friction. Ground rays capture tire-center anchors and the loaded side's contact line becomes the body pivot. While roll is below 40 degrees, the two loaded wheel bones counter wrapper rotation and translation: their centers stay at the captured world anchors, and their tire orientation follows the terrain frame. Opposite wheels lift with the body. Loaded wheels ease out of their anchors between 40 and 55 degrees, then fully release; wheel vertex support then prevents floor penetration without forcing all four tires to stay planted. Body support uses rest mesh vertices during reactions and eight body-only bounds corners during ordinary driving; tire support stays independent of body lifting. All support is against the sampled terrain plane, with 2 cm clearance. Ordinary driving remains slope-aligned with eased acceleration/braking pitch, height bounce and signed steering roll.

## Friction, tips and disabled rollover

Light impacts below 5 m/s use the same eased contact-anchored tip phase as medium hits, scaled to a maximum 14-degree combined pitch/roll tip. A 3 m/s hit is about 7 degrees at default grip. The tip phase lasts 0.3 seconds, then a more tightly damped spring recovers with a small rock-back. Repeated light hits replace a capped target instead of accumulating rollover energy; both pitch and roll are capped and rollover energy is zero, guaranteeing recovery once hits cease.

Medium hits (5 to below 12 m/s) now use the actual car-local push to choose torque: pitch = local Z, roll = -local X. Front/rear hits pitch around the front/rear loaded tires, lateral hits roll around the loaded side, and diagonals blend axes and load the corresponding tire corner. There is no longitudinal-to-roll fallback. Pure vertical grounded hits bounce without inventing horizontal torque; genuinely ambiguous vertical airborne tumble still uses small random axes. Model yaw corrections are part of mesh mapping, not an extra flip applied to velocity or aim.

Angular strength is continuous through the light/medium boundary: below 5 m/s the light angle grows toward the 14-degree cap; from 5 to 11 m/s the medium target blends from 14 to 120 degrees before grip/slope bias, capped at 120. Default flat-ground grip gives about 57 degrees at 8 m/s and 102 at 11 m/s. Controlled rotation uses one axis-angle transform rather than competing Euler roll/pitch phases. Wheels release according to total tip angle, including pitch and diagonals.

Rollover is now geometry-based, replacing the 88-degree/energy/random-resistance gate. The estimated center of mass is the model-scaled chassis pivot at 40% of body height. Its gravity projection onto the terrain plane is compared with the loaded tire support point/line in the push direction. Once that projection crosses support by 1 cm with outward angular motion above 0.15 rad/s, a medium tip continues into DISABLED rather than springing upright. Continuation preserves outward angular speed, accelerates/decelerates toward the side/roof with a 4 rad/s target-speed cap, and never rewinds toward upright. Below-balance tips recover. Side/roof settling follows the same directional axis; larger target rotations continue onto the roof. This is a geometry/kinematics approximation, not measured model mass distribution or a complete evolving support-polygon solver. Light classification remains capped and cannot trigger rollover; charged taps can graduate into medium classification.

A qualifying grounded rollover enters DISABLED, stops movement/recovery and eases onto a side or roof. Wheel/body support keeps the visual above the ground; collision layers and masks become zero immediately, and the driving shape is disabled before the next physics step. Reset remains available. There is no explosion for grounded rollover. Sufficiently energetic impacts at 12+ still launch engine-controlled ballistic flight with gravity, signed randomized mixed pitch/roll spin, and a fixed world-axis-aligned box tracking the animated center. Flight sweep/overlap checks exclude self and explode/hide once on terrain or building contact. Ordinary road contact does not explode a driving or grounded disabled car.

## Limits and cost

This is a contact-aware procedural prototype, not a rigid-body tire simulation. Tire friction is modeled through anchoring, tip resistance/bias and movement suppression, without a tire slip solver. Independent wheel bones may visibly stretch their connection to the chassis during a deep tip. Contact transfer after loaded tires release can slide; the no-slip guarantee applies to the loaded contact phase. No wheel spin/steering animation is added. Planar support passes on the flat road/ramp; sharp edges, curbs, discontinuous multi-wheel terrain and nearby building contacts during a grounded tip need additional probes/collision work. Grounded DISABLED cars are pass-through; visual support still uses the sampled plane. The root is fixed only during the initially planted tire phase. Disabled cars reject driving and new pushes, but continue their existing passive rollover motion until frictional rest.

Sampling rest mesh vertices and updating wheel bones adds CPU work compared with the prior wrapper-only demo. The tick counter excludes rendering/engine physics and is not a benchmark; no fleet performance saving is claimed. Production route integration, truck hit forwarding, swept chain hits and server snapshots are now included. No deformation or complete tire/contact physics solver is included.

## Grounded disabled cue and removal

The demo and production Kenney traffic share hybrid_disabled_cue.gd. Entering grounded DISABLED immediately removes collision layers/masks and defers shape disabling, adds a yellow inverted-hull overlay and twelve low-poly CPU smoke puffs near the engine, and hides production vehicle lights. Recoverable light/medium reactions and strong flight do not receive this cue. Repeated updates never recreate it.

After passive rollover settles, defaults are a 3-second hold and a 2-second smoothstep fade, configurable on each car. Body, yellow outline and smoke fade together, then the standalone actor or production owner is removed. Each car duplicates its active materials; imported resources and nearby cars remain unchanged. Original material transparency is preserved during the hold, then per-material alpha provides Compatibility-renderer fading. Reset creates a fresh collision-enabled car. The demo safely handles removal and keeps reset/model controls available.

Server snapshots include cue age and hold/fade durations. Clients replay the authoritative opacity and emitter location, including late joins halfway through the fade; repeated snapshots do not duplicate the emitter. Particle random positions are cosmetic and are not transmitted. Authoritative missing-prop cleanup removes client actors. Strong explosion cleanup stops and frees any cue.

## Production truck landing explosion launch

Truck slide contacts now evaluate pre-move_and_slide velocity against the impacted car's motion_vector. A qualifying landing needs an upward-facing contact normal (Y >= 0.7), a contact point in the top portion of the actual car collision box and within its horizontal footprint, downward relative velocity of at least 3 m/s, and at least 3 m/s approach along the normal. Mere height difference, slow resting support, side impacts and underside hits do not qualify. Grounded disabled cars remain pass-through and do not qualify; a live or airborne car can be consumed.

A successful server-owned landing consumes the car through its one-shot explosion flow, clears pending hybrid hits and freezes the owner. Existing explosion smoke/removal and replicated exploded state are reused. Truck launch is set to 12 m/s upward plus up to 14 m/s horizontally, not added cumulatively. Inspector settings under Car Landing Launch configure threshold, upward/lateral speed and cooldown (default 0.8 seconds); hard caps are 25 m/s upward and 30 m/s laterally. Already exploded cars cannot launch again even after the cooldown. The horizontal launch is maintained until ground landing, with existing gravity and collision handling, and reset clears it. Terrain hill-launch logic cannot overwrite the car launch.

Direction uses existing accelerate/reverse and left/right actions at contact, transformed by truck yaw: RT forward, LT backward, left-stick X left/right, or W/S/A/D and arrow keys. Trigger and stick action deadzones remain those of the production InputMap/GameOptions. Analog components are retained and the combined vector is limited to unit length. Forward+turn produces a diagonal; reverse+turn combines backward and the pressed lateral direction. Steering alone chooses that side. Opposing inputs cancel; neutral or disabled manual input gives upward-only launch. Right-stick camera inputs are untouched. Direction is sampled at impact; this does not add midair aim controls or controller remapping.

Existing traffic collision police reporting and truck damage factor (relative downward speed times 0.6, through armor/damage handling) are retained. Multiplayer uses sanitized remote driving actions on the server, existing truck transform/velocity fields, and existing one-shot car explosion snapshot replay. Clients do not author landing explosions or launches.

Reproduce in actual gameplay: F5 the production project, approach a raised road/crest with a traffic car below, and land the truck onto its roof while falling at least 3 m/s. Hold RT plus left-stick X (or W+A/D) to choose forward diagonal launch; LT plus stick chooses reverse diagonal, stick alone chooses a side, and release drive inputs for upward-only. Side bumps preserve normal reactions. For a repeatable automated production collision fixture, run Godot --headless --path <Food_Truck_Drive> --script res://tools/car_landing_launch_test.gd.

## Advancing grounded rollover

Grounded rollover no longer rotates at one fixed world position. The loaded tire phase remains anchored while total tip is below 40 degrees and releases smoothly from 40 to 55 degrees, including after balance crossing into DISABLED. At 55 degrees the shared controller releases the original contact pivot and rebases the owning root without a horizontal visual jump. Each subsequent tick rotates about the previous lowest body/tire support point, transfers to the new lowest support, and advances the root in the actual world push direction. Mesh vertices determine support rather than one fixed chassis pivot.

Medium impacts retain horizontal momentum from 0.32 times relative impact velocity plus 0.35 times preimpact movement, limited to 7 m/s. The production braking queue now saves preimpact movement before traction braking. A minimum forward push component prevents opposing prior motion from reversing the rollover. Charge/release strength automatically scales both existing angular targets and this retained momentum. Friction slows momentum at 2 + 2*tire_friction m/s squared; geometric rolling travel remains tied to the changing support. Total passive displacement is capped at 12 meters and each tick at 0.3 meters. A center-height ray stops translation into steep obstacle faces; this is not a swept vehicle hull.

DISABLED still switches off driving and player collision immediately, rejects further hits and never explodes from a grounded medium rollover. It retains pending passive movement through balance crossing. The yellow outline/smoke starts on transition, stays fully visible during travel, then begins its full 3-second hold plus 2-second fade after angular motion and horizontal momentum settle. Settled wrecks avoid repeating the support-vertex search. Light hits still recover and do not gain travel momentum; strong flight and the production roof-landing explosion launch are retained.

Ground rays use a saved world collision mask after player pass-through sets the actor's mask to zero. Samples follow the traveled root, adjust height with the new terrain plane and update slope-aligned visual/body/tire support. The solver remains a sampled-plane approximation: curbs, sharp edges, changing multi-wheel terrain and building-side contacts can need a full swept hull/contact solver. Frictional slip is intentionally added to geometric contact transfer; it is not a complete no-slip rigid-body dynamics simulation.

Production snapshots now include the moved root transform, current travel velocity, retained momentum, accumulated distance and settled flag, plus existing visual/wheel poses and cue age. Clients apply the server state and never simulate their own rollover or explosion. Existing outer prop transforms remain synchronized as well.

# City generation

The earlier generator used a fixed 4-by-5 block grid, fixed one-way streets and diagonal road templates. The new plan keeps grid-aligned geometry while selecting the playable boundary, highway exits, connected straight arteries, inner streets and land use from a seed.

Small maps have 3-by-4 cells; Large maps have 4-by-5. Each boundary has an independently sampled 14–22 metre outer margin, configurable with `boundary_margin_min` and `boundary_margin_max`. Three to five exits are spaced around the boundary. Only selected highway roads extend outside it. Block merges of two or three cells occur only along city edges; interior blocks stay separate.

Exit arteries connect through central arteries. Main arteries have two driving lanes in each direction. Street-level arteries have curb parking as a third lane and traffic signals, without stop signs or separate street lamps. Bridge arteries have a hatched breakdown lane, street lamps and Kenney bridge decks/pillars, without parking bays or traffic signals. Joining roads rise gradually using `bridge_ramp_length`; `bridge_clearance` sets deck height. This is a connected elevated road network, rather than separate stacked roads passing through each other.

Inner streets mix one-way and two-way routes. A connectivity check prevents one-way choices from isolating parts of the city. Gray plots receive buildings; isolated green plots receive a few buildings and fences. Adjacent green plots become parks. Edge parks receive an impassable billboard forest; other edges receive simple cliff barriers, leaving highway openings clear.

Generation builds the plan, roads, buildings/plot surfaces, parks, sidewalks, bridge details and parking, then lights, signs and furniture. Furniture placement checks completed sidewalk contours. Visual smoothing follows generation while collision stays simple. The resulting geometry is saved per map size and included in client updates by `Publish_Update.ps1 -Build`.

Validation: `tools/city_layout_plan_test.gd` checks 600 seeded Small/Large plans. `tools/city_layout_integration_test.gd` loads both shipped cities, checks lighting/signage placement and drives through every highway exit. Rebuild snapshots with `tools/bake_city.gd -- --force --map-size 0` and again with size `1` after generator changes.

The main menu now has an **Original grid (no bridge arteries)** toggle. Enable it before Play Saved City or Generate New City to use separate regular rectangular blocks and ground-level roads; disable it for the current artery plan. The toggle restores the old grid orientation, while retaining the current map sizes, spaced highway exits, scenery and driving systems. Multiplayer hosts can select Original grid in the lobby, and that selection is sent to every peer. Grid saves and shipped Small/Large snapshots have separate paths, so switching layouts does not overwrite the other city. Build updates bake all four size/layout variants. To rebuild a grid snapshot use `tools/bake_city.gd -- --map-size 0 --original-grid --force` (or size 1).

The loading screen draws the selected city's actual boundary, exits, arteries/streets, plots, sidewalk outlines and furniture stages. Saved cities replay these disclosures while their containers are attached. The completed diagram zooms to the local truck's spawn over 1.2 seconds, dissolves for 1.2 seconds, and overlaps a 3.2-second overhead-to-chase camera interpolation with smooth acceleration and deceleration. Truck input and the HUD resume after the camera arrives. Animated checker/stripe shaders remain in place. This does not change the running editor's frame cap or enable replay recording.

Gameplay still starts after the whole saved snapshot is instantiated and attached. Existing distance-based car sprites and building boxes reduce rendered detail, but do not unload those resources. True early play would require splitting saved geometry into block chunks, loading the spawn neighborhood and safety perimeter first, then scheduling nearby chunks ahead of the truck. Collision, highway transitions and multiplayer-authoritative actors must be ready before a player reaches each chunk. Fog can conceal the visual detail transition; it cannot replace collision readiness.
